Place + time = experience

History is mostly something you read. Books, Wikipedia, old photographs, museums. But people understand a thing far better when they can put themselves inside it. This turns history from something you read into somewhere you can go. You answer two questions, where am I and when am I, and the world is rebuilt around you.

  1. Step 01

    Search a place

  2. Step 02

    Choose a year

    Drag the timeline back. Today to 1850. Then 1666. Then, in Rome, 100 AD.

  3. Step 03

    Press Travel

    You arrive, and you walk. Six stops, forty two minutes, each one standing where the thing it describes stood.

What arrives with you

What gets rebuilt around you.

Asking a chatbot gets you “tell me about London in 1850”. This is “take me to London in 1850”. You are standing on the corner, and these are the questions it answers for you.

The streets
What stood on this corner, and what it was called then.
The buildings
Which were here, which were not built yet, which had already gone.
Clothes
What people wore to work, and what that told everyone about them.
Getting about
On foot, by omnibus, by rail, by boat, and how long it took.
Trades
Who sold what on this street, and who bought it.
Food
What was eaten, what was in season, what was safe.
Prices
What a loaf, a room and a day of labour cost.
People
Who lived here, how many, and how they were counted.
Events
What was happening that year, on this street and in the country.
The air
Noise, smell, smoke and weather. What the street felt like to walk.

Historical confidence

History isn’t always certain.

The further back you travel, the less the record says, and we do not pretend otherwise. Every line is marked with how sure it is: known from the record, likely from strong context, or the model’s best interpretation. The mark sits on the words themselves, not in a footnote.

Fleet Street is the centre of the London newspaper trade. The Daily News and the Standard are printed within a few hundred yards of where you are standing. The air is thick with coal smoke, and the road is jammed with omnibuses, carts and cabs. On the corner a boy sells the morning edition from a wooden crate, and shouts the headline twice.

Written down, not promised

Where this is going.

None of the following is in the first release, and none of it has a date. It is written here so you can see the shape of the thing rather than be surprised by it later.

  • Ask the past

    Put a question to the year you are standing in. What would dinner cost, who owns this building, was this street rich or poor.

  • Meet someone

    Dickens in 1850. A merchant in Rome in 100 AD. Answers from inside that life, not above it.

  • What happened here

    The full timeline of one spot. Your office was farmland, then a medieval house, then this.

  • Compare

    London 1850 beside London today. Or London 1850 beside Tokyo 1850.

  • Follow a person

    Travel through a life instead of a place, and let the map move as they move.

  • Travel between places

    London to New York in 1850. No aeroplane. The real ship, the real cost, the real weeks at sea.
